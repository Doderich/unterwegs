Privacy policy

Last updated: 7 November 2026 · app version 1.5.0

In short

  • Unterwegs - Travel Log is an app for your own, self-hosted AdventureLog server. Your travel data lives on that server, not with the app developer.
  • The developer provides only the app (the client). They run no public AdventureLog server and own neither the server you connect to nor the data you put in it; the operator of your server is responsible for that.
  • The app developer receives no travel data, no credentials and no locations.
  • No ads, no third-party analytics or tracking services, no data sold or shared for advertising.
  • The app reports technical data to the developer's update server (installation ID, device model, operating system, crash flags). Crash reports can be turned off in the app. Details below.

1. Controller

For the data the app sends to the developer's update server (section 3), the controller is __CONTROLLER_NAME__, __CONTROLLER_ADDRESS__, email: __CONTACT_EMAIL__.

The developer provides only the app, the client. They do not own or operate the AdventureLog server you connect to, and not the data you put in it. Your travel data lives on the AdventureLog server you choose; the operator of that server is responsible for it (section 2). That can be you or someone who runs the server for you.

2. Your AdventureLog server

The app connects only to the server whose address you enter (or take from a QR code). It loads your collections there, with locations, lodging, transportation, notes, photos, PDFs and coordinates, and writes your changes back. The server operator's privacy rules apply there. The app developer has no access to that server.

The connection uses HTTPS only. Your API key is sent as a header to the signed-in server only, never to other hosts.

On the sign-in screen, the app offers a shortcut to the public test server demo.adventurelog.app. It is not operated by this app's developer; do not upload personal data there.

3. What the developer's update server receives

The app can load JavaScript updates over the air (OTA) from a self-hosted update server and report health data to it. The versions in the App Store and Google Play do this.

  • Update check on every start: installation ID (random UUID, not an account or hardware identifier), app version, platform, channel, running update ID, update-load failure signals. As with any internet connection, the server also sees your IP address.
  • Start signal (“app_started”): a fixed event name with no content, once per launch, together with device model, operating system version and app version.
  • Crash reports: for fatal JavaScript or native errors, the error message and stack trace, and a crash flag for the installation. The app adds no account or device names, coordinates, server addresses, collection content or request content.

The installation ID and device details are pseudonymous, not anonymous. There is no product-usage statistics, no screen tracking and no network tracing. No Expo/EAS hosted service is used; the data goes directly to the self-hosted server. If reporting fails or you are offline, the app opens normally. Purpose: delivering updates and finding out whether a version crashes (legitimate interest, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Retention: the update server keeps one record per installation (installation ID, first and last contact, running update ID, device model, operating system, app version, crash flag with a bounded error message). It deletes these records 90 days after the installation was last seen, and deletes them at once on request (section 9). No geolocation of the IP address is configured. Objection: in the app under Settings → Privacy → “Send crash reports” you can turn off crash and start reports; pending reports are then discarded. The update check itself (installation ID, app version, running update) continues because it is needed to deliver updates; you can object to that by email (section 9).

4. Other services that are contacted

  • Exchange rates: When Frankfurter is selected, opening the calculator, changing currencies or reconnecting refreshes the rate automatically. You can also refresh manually. The app requests the currency pair from api.frankfurter.dev. Amounts, collection data and credentials are not sent. The connection technically transmits your IP address. Provider privacy and usage information.
  • Map tiles and map style: tiles.openfreemap.org (OpenFreeMap, map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). When you view or download a map, this service sees your IP address and, through the requested tiles, which map area you look at. Privacy and terms: OpenFreeMap privacy policy, terms of service.
  • Wikimedia: in “Photo from the web”, the app loads preview images directly from upload.wikimedia.org. Wikimedia sees your IP address.
  • Services your server uses: place search (Nominatim/OpenStreetMap), descriptions and images from Wikipedia, places nearby (Overpass/OpenStreetMap, optionally Google if your operator set it up), Immich and Strava. Your server makes these requests, not the app; your server operator's privacy policy applies. For the Strava connection, the app opens Strava's sign-in page; the app does not see your Strava credentials.
  • Links you tap: map apps (Apple Maps, Google Maps, Organic Maps, OsmAnd) and booking sites (booking.com) open only when you tap a link and are subject to their own privacy rules.

5. Permissions

  • Camera: to scan the sign-in QR code and to take photos for your trip. No microphone.
  • Photos: only when you pick images; they are uploaded to the location, lodging or transportation on your server.
  • Location (while in use): only when you create a place at your current position. The coordinates go to your server (and through it to its geocoding service), not to the developer. No background location.
  • Notifications: only when you turn on reminders. The app schedules them itself on the device from your offline copy; there are no push notifications and no service that sends them.

6. What is stored on your device

  • Unit choices and time zones are saved on the device. Travel essentials you enter (contacts, insurance details and phrases) stay in the local database and are not sent to your server or an external service. Delete them in Travel essentials or by choosing to sign out and delete everything.
  • Currency choices and manual and fetched exchange rates stay on the device as device-wide preferences, including after sign-out. Choosing “Save conversion” also stores the amount, result, currency pair and rate used for the last 100 conversions on this device only. Clear this history in the calculator, or by choosing to sign out and delete everything.
  • Server address and API key in the operating system's secure storage (iOS Keychain, only when unlocked and only on this device; Android Keystore-backed storage).
  • A copy of your collections and other loaded data in a local database (SQLite) so the app starts offline; also settings.
  • Downloaded files (PDFs, photos), image cache, offline map areas and exports, kept separately per server.
  • Android: backup is turned off. iOS: offline files and the database can be part of your own device backup; the API key is not.
  • For widgets and the travel-day display on the Lock Screen (iOS), the app keeps the text they show and the cover photo of the next collection in a shared area on the device. Booking numbers and prices are never part of it. Signing out empties it.

7. Signing out and deleting

  • Sign out: the key is deleted from the device and cached data is removed. The app asks whether to keep or delete downloaded files, photos and maps. Optionally the key is also deleted on the server.
  • Delete account: in the app under Settings → Account (deletes the account on the server, then all local data). See Delete account.
  • Uninstalling the app removes all of its local data.

8. Children

The app's content is suitable for everyone (store rating “Everyone” / 4+). The app is, however, not directed at children under 13; its target audience is users aged 13 and over. The developer does not knowingly collect data from children under 13.

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, data portability and objection, and the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority. For travel data, contact the operator of your server. For the data in section 3, write to __CONTACT_EMAIL__. The app does not show the installation ID; instead name your device, operating system and roughly when you used the app, so the record can be found.

10. Changes

If the app changes (for example new services), this policy is updated; the date above shows the version.
